Engineering

How Witbound syncs an audiobook to its ebook, entirely on your phone

I wanted Whispersync for files I already own — read the page while the narrator reads it aloud, every word highlighted in time. Nothing did it for books that didn't come from one specific store. So I built it, and set myself one rule: it has to run entirely on the device, with no account and nothing uploaded. Here's how it works.

The problem is alignment, not transcription

An audiobook and its ebook are the same words in two forms — but nothing connects them. The audio has no idea which word is being spoken at 1:12:04, and the text has no idea when word number 41,900 gets read aloud. To highlight the page in time with the voice, you need a map: for every word in the book, the moment it's spoken.

The naïve approach — force-align the whole audio against the whole text — is brittle. Real pairs never match cleanly. Editions differ. The ebook has front matter, a licence page, chapter titles the narrator skips. The audiobook has an intro, a "chapter one" the text doesn't mark, an outro. Abridgements drop whole passages. A rigid aligner drifts a few seconds early and never recovers, and the highlight ends up a sentence ahead of the voice — worse than no highlight at all.

Two stages: transcribe, then diff

Witbound splits it in two. First, iOS 26's on-device SpeechAnalyzer transcribes the audiobook with per-word timings. This is the part that used to require a server — Apple's new speech model is good enough, and fast enough, to do it on the phone. The model downloads once; after that everything is local.

Second, a patience-diff-style n-gram aligner matches the transcript against the tokenized ebook. Instead of forcing a global fit, it finds confident anchor points — rare n-grams that appear once in both — and aligns between them, the same idea git diff uses to line up code. Where the transcript and text genuinely diverge (a skipped heading, a dropped paragraph), the run between anchors simply stays unmatched, and that text renders as ordinary un-narrated prose rather than dragging the whole map out of sync. Robustness comes from being willing not to match.

It's fast enough to be boring

On an iPhone 15 Pro the transcription runs 50–80× faster than realtime — a 90-minute chapter aligns in about 90 seconds; a 39-minute one in under 30. When I first measured that on real hardware I assumed I'd made an error, because the whole architecture had been designed around the assumption this would need a server. It doesn't. A full-length novel processes in a few minutes, once, and never again.

Accuracy held up better than I expected too. On a 37.6-hour production audiobook the median word-boundary error was about 0.06 seconds — well below what the eye can catch as "off." As a stress test I aligned an 850,000-word book against its narration and got zero mismatched anchors end to end.

Checkpoints, so you can read before it finishes

Transcription is chunked by chapter, and each chapter's transcript is written to disk the moment it completes. Two things fall out of that. First, the sync map is rebuilt after every chapter, so you can start reading as soon as chapter one aligns while the rest catches up behind you — the unfinished part just reads as un-narrated until it's ready. Second, the work is resumable: if iOS suspends the app, or you close it, nothing already transcribed is lost. An overnight BGProcessingTask finishes long books while the phone charges, then sends a notification when the book is ready.

On-device on principle, not just for privacy

Everything above runs on the phone. There's no account, no sign-up, no server — the App Store privacy label reads Data Not Collected, because there's nothing to collect. That started as a privacy stance and turned into an engineering constraint that made the product better: no upload wait, no per-minute transcription bill, works on a plane.

The one thing it deliberately won't do is open DRM'd files. Audible and Kindle files are copy-protected, and breaking that protection is illegal — so Witbound refuses them up front with a clear message rather than failing strangely later. DRM-free epub/PDF and m4b/mp3 work: Libro.fm, LibriVox, Standard Ebooks, your own rips.
