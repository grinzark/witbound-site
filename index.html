How it works

Bring an ebook and its audiobook. Your phone does the rest.

Two files you already own become one book you can hear and see. The matching happens on your iPhone or iPad, and your books are never uploaded.

  1. i.

    Add both halves

    An epub (Kobo .kepub works too) or a PDF, and the audiobook as m4b, m4a, mp3, flac, wav or aiff. A folder of parts becomes one book, with a chapter for each part.

  2. ii.

    Your iPhone matches them, once

    It goes through the narration and lines every spoken word up with the page. You can start reading when chapter one is done. The rest finishes in the background, overnight while it charges if you like, and a notification tells you when it’s ready. If your iPhone gets warm, it rests for a minute or two, then carries on by itself.

  3. iii.

    Press play

    The narrator reads and each word lights up on the book’s own pages. Tap any word and the audio jumps there. Your place is kept to the word.

One catch. Audible and Kindle-store books won’t open. They’re copy-protected, and Witbound won’t break that. DRM-free books work: Libro.fm, LibriVox, Standard Ebooks and Project Gutenberg. New to this? Start with the free public-domain pairs.

Word by word

Every word lights up as it’s spoken.

The highlight follows the voice a word at a time, on the publisher’s own pages: italics, small caps, illustrations and maps all where they belong. Tap a word to send the narrator there.

Light a single word, a phrase, a sentence or the whole paragraph, in indigo, warm gold, teal or rose. A reading window can dim everything but the lines being read, and the highlight can run a beat ahead of the voice, for readers who like to be ahead. Both are off until you turn them on.

Pause and keep reading with your eyes; press play and the narrator starts where you are. At the end of a long day the controls fade while you listen, the sleep timer only counts while the book is playing, and play picks up a few seconds before where you stopped, further back after a long break.

The page

The whole book, pictures and all.

Plates and maps stay where the publisher put them. Tap an illustration to see it full screen and zoom in.

Set the page the way you read. Choose the font, the size, how wide the text runs and how much room sits between the lines, and the text can be justified or left aligned. One of the four fonts is Atkinson Hyperlegible, drawn for readers with low vision, and text goes up to 40 points. Read on Paper, Sepia or Night, which is for the last chapter in bed, with the screen turned down.

Some nights you only want the page. Press and hold a book and choose Read without audio, or tap Read only on the player while you listen. There’s no player on screen, a tap turns the page, and nothing plays until you ask for the narrator again. When you want the voice back, tap the small headphones under the page: the narrator starts on the page you’re reading, and the page stays where it is.

Press and hold, then choose Highlight or Add Note; to select a passage, tap a sentence or drag across exactly the words you want. A highlight keeps its punctuation, from the opening quote mark to the full stop. Bookmarks, highlights and notes stay with the book, and every book keeps them in a notebook: search it, tap an entry to jump to that page and that moment in the audio, and export it as text or Markdown.

Your library

Your books, from wherever they already live.

Plain files work, and so do the servers you already run. Nothing is copied anywhere you didn’t choose.

Bring an ebook and its audio from the Files app, iCloud Drive or AirDrop. Or connect your own audiobook server, at home, over Tailscale or with an API key, and browse it with covers and narrators. Your place syncs both ways, so the server’s other apps pick up where you stopped.

Pair the audiobook with the ebook from your own OPDS catalogue, import from your media server, or let Witbound for Mac send a book over your own Wi-Fi, already synced. No ebook at all? Witbound offers to find it in your library first. If there isn’t one, it writes the book from the narration, chapter by chapter.

Discover is a tab beside your Library. Its free classics are twenty public-domain books with their LibriVox recordings, already synced, and they never use up your free book. Its Synced list shows editions and narrations that sync almost instantly. Witbound doesn’t provide books: get your own copies of that edition and narration, and syncing is almost instant. Tap a book to read a short description of it, when Open Library has one.

Which servers? Support lists the ones that work.

Groups

Read a book with your friends.

Start a group, pick a book you’ve synced, and invite friends with a code. Everyone reads their own copy, in their own edition, and the group keeps pace together.

The owner splits the book into stages that end at chapters, each with a date to reach it by. When a reader clears a stage, the group sees it. The track shows where every reader is, from the start to the end, and who’s reading right now. A different edition is matched to the owner’s chapters.

Highlight a passage and share it with the group. Friends see it in their own copy once they reach that chapter, so nothing is spoiled, and can play it in their own narrator’s voice or react with one of four emoji. Only where the passage is goes to the group, never its words or your notes.

If the group turns on Read or die, missing a stage’s date puts you out of the group until it starts its next book, unless the owner lets you back in. The owner plays by the same rule. No money, ever: just pride.

Send friends the 8-letter code or the link. They type the code, or paste the link, in the Groups tab.

On every screen

One book, on the screens you read on.

Witbound is for iPhone and iPad, with a free companion for the Mac that does the slow part, and an app for Android on Google Play.

Your iPhone and iPad

Your place in each EPUB book follows you between your own iPhone and iPad through your iCloud, with no account. Open the book on one and Witbound offers to continue from where the other got to, by the device’s name. Your place stays in your own iCloud and never reaches Witbound. Both need the same ebook and the same Apple Account, and there’s an off switch in Settings.

Your e-reader

Set up any KOReader sync server in Settings and your place goes both ways. Come back from your e-reader and Witbound offers to continue from it, by its name, then lands on that page and the nearest point in the narration. Go the other way and your e-reader opens on the page you were reading, not at the start of the chapter.

What leaves your phone

What leaves your phone, and what never does.

There’s no account, no ads and no analytics that track you. One feature talks to Witbound by default, community sync, and it shares timings, not books.

Stays on your device

  • Your ebooks, and your audiobooks too unless you turn on Cloud sync on Android
  • Your library and your reading stats
  • Highlights, notes and bookmarks, unless you share them, or send them to a library server of yours that takes highlights
  • Your settings

Goes online, and when

  • Community sync: word timings and fingerprints of the book, never its text, title or audio. There’s an off switch in Settings.
  • On Android, Cloud sync, if you turn it on: an English audiobook’s audio, in five-minute pieces over Wi-Fi, to Witbound’s server, which turns it into timed words and deletes it. Never your ebook.
  • Your place in each book, to your own iCloud, so your iPhone and iPad can pick up where the other left off. Witbound never sees it, and there’s an off switch in Settings.
  • Servers you connect yourself: your own audiobook server, ebook catalogue or media server, or a KOReader sync server (which ones).
  • Your purchase, through Apple or Google Play.
  • Anything you choose to share: a quote, a note, a progress card.
  • Discover, when you use it: the public Wanted and Synced lists and your searches in them, book covers and short descriptions, a book you add (looked up in Open Library or Google Books) and the free classics you get, from Project Gutenberg and the Internet Archive.
  • Your to-read list, and a book you’ve synced, only if you choose to share them.

  1. Your book and its audio

    Stay on the device. (On Android, Cloud sync sends the audio only if you turn it on, then deletes it.)

  3. A timing map

    Word positions and times. No text, no audio.

  5. The next reader with the same edition

    Their copy is ready in seconds.

If someone has already synced your edition, your copy is ready in seconds. Since version 1.8.2 Witbound also recognises the same book when the files aren’t identical: a friend’s copy, a different epub edition, or audio that was re-encoded. When your iPhone checks a shared sync against your own audio, it goes chapter by chapter, shows how far it has got, and lets you read the chapters it has checked. When you tap Share on a book you’ve synced, it’s sent straight away, without waiting on the online catalogue, and once it’s listed, the next reader with that edition sees it on Discover’s Synced list.

On the App Store, Witbound’s privacy label lists Identifiers, Usage Data, User Content and Other Data as Data Linked to You, and Search History as not linked. What’s linked is Groups, and only if you join one: your group name and picture, a device key in place of an account, your place in the group’s book, the quotes you share there (where they are in the book, never its words), and the groups and books you add. Community sync’s timing maps and Discover’s lists still don’t say who you are. The privacy policy says exactly what is sent, and where.

Questions

The questions people ask first.

Something else? Support has more, or email me.

Will my Audible or Kindle books work?

No, and they never will. They’re copy-protected, and Witbound won’t break that. DRM-free books work: Libro.fm, LibriVox, Standard Ebooks and Project Gutenberg. New to this? Start with the free pairs.

What do I need?

An iPhone or iPad on iOS 26 or later, an ebook (epub or PDF) and its audiobook (m4b, m4a, mp3, flac, wav or aiff). Or connect your own audiobook server, ebook catalogue or media server: which servers work. On Android 8 or later: an epub and its audiobook as m4b, m4a or mp3, from your files, Google Drive or the same servers.

How long does the first sync take?

You can start reading once chapter one is done, and the rest finishes in the background, overnight if you like. Popular editions are ready in seconds through community sync. The free Mac app syncs a long book in minutes.

What if my ebook and audiobook are different editions?

Witbound matches what it can and leaves the rest as ordinary text. It won’t force a bad match.

Do I need the internet?

Not to read or listen. Witbound goes online for community sync, which you can switch off, for Discover when you use it, for servers you connect, for purchases, and on Android for Cloud sync if you turn it on.

Which languages?

English, French, Spanish, German, Italian, Portuguese, Japanese, Korean and Chinese, worked out from the book. Polish and more through Apple’s dictation, where the highlight can land a beat off more often. English is measured across 32 books. Of the languages added in 1.8.0 I’ve measured one, Polish, on one book.

Where are my books stored?

On your device. Your ebooks are never uploaded, apart from a short passage you choose to share. On Android, if you turn on Cloud sync, an audiobook’s audio goes to Witbound’s server to be turned into timed words, and is deleted straight after. The privacy policy has the details.

A note from Moe

I read the same books twice: ears on the commute, eyes at night. Every switch meant scanning pages for my place. Witbound is the app I wanted for that, and it’s just me making it, in the UK.
