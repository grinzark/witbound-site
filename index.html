00:49−26:26

Bring an ebook and its audiobook. Your phone does the rest.

Two files you already own become one book you can hear and see. The matching happens on your iPhone or iPad, and your books are never uploaded.

  1. 1

    Add both halves

    An epub (Kobo .kepub works too) or a PDF, and the audiobook as m4b, m4a, mp3, flac, wav or aiff. A folder of parts becomes one book, with a chapter for each part.

  2. 2

    Your iPhone matches them, once

    It goes through the narration and lines every spoken word up with the page. You can start reading when chapter one is done. The rest finishes in the background, overnight while it charges if you like, and a notification tells you when it’s ready.

  3. 3

    Press play

    The narrator reads and each word lights up on the book’s own pages. Tap any word and the audio jumps there. Your place is kept to the word.

One catch. Audible and Kindle-store books won’t open. They’re copy-protected, and Witbound won’t break that. DRM-free books work: Libro.fm, LibriVox, Standard Ebooks and Project Gutenberg. New to this? Start with the free public-domain pairs.

Every word lights up as it’s spoken.

The highlight follows the voice a word at a time, on the publisher’s own pages: italics, small caps, illustrations and maps all where they belong. Tap a word to send the narrator there.

Ways to follow along

Highlight
A word, a phrase, a sentence or the whole paragraph.
Colour
Indigo, warm gold, teal or rose.
Reading window
Dims everything except the lines being read.
Lead the narrator
Lights the next word a beat early, for readers who like to be ahead.
Bold word-starts
The start of each word in bold, to give the eye an anchor.
Scroll or pages
One continuous scroll, or turn page by page.
Silent reading
Pause and keep reading with your eyes. Press play and the narrator starts where you are.
Tap and hold
A word’s meaning or a translation, without moving the audio.

Set the page the way you read.

Since version 1.8.5 you choose the font, the size, how wide the text runs and how much room sits between the lines. Then mark it up: bookmarks, highlights and notes stay with the book.

Four fonts

  • Georgia
  • New York
  • San Francisco
  • Atkinson Hyperlegible

Atkinson Hyperlegible was drawn for readers with low vision. Text goes up to 40 points.

Three widths

On iPad, Full lets the text fill the screen. Line and paragraph spacing are yours to set as well.

Three papers

Night is for the last chapter in bed, with the screen turned down.

  • Bookmarks

    Bookmark a page from the top bar. Contents lists them, and one tap brings back the page and the narration together.

  • Highlights and notes

    Press and hold, then choose Highlight or Add Note. Highlights show on the page, and tapping one opens its note.

  • Select a passage

    Tap a sentence, or drag across exactly the words you want to keep.

  • Take it with you

    Export highlights and notes as text, save passages to Obsidian as linked notes, or share a quote to your Discord book club with the narrator’s own reading attached.

  • Read without audio

    Some nights you only want the page. Press and hold a book and choose Read without audio, or tap Read only on the player while you listen. There’s no player on screen, a tap turns the page, and nothing plays until you ask for the narrator again. The book opens that way until you do.

  • Keep screen on

    The screen stays awake while a book is open, and sleeps again after five minutes without a touch. It’s on from the start; you’ll find it under Reading in Settings, and Low Power Mode turns it off.

Your books, from wherever they already live.

Plain files work, and so do the servers you already run. Nothing is copied anywhere you didn’t choose.

Files
An ebook and its audio from the Files app, iCloud Drive or AirDrop.
Your audiobook server
Connect at home, over Tailscale or with an API key. Browse, search and sort with covers and narrators, whole libraries included. Your place syncs both ways, so the server’s other apps pick up where you stopped.
Your ebook catalogue
Pair an audiobook with the ebook from your own OPDS catalogue, found by title and author, and browse it by author, series or shelf where the catalogue offers them.
Your media server
Sign in or type your server’s address, then import audiobooks from your music library.
Your Mac
Witbound for Mac sends books over your own Wi-Fi, already synced.
Audio only
No ebook? Witbound offers to find it in your library first. If there isn’t one, it writes the book from the narration, chapter by chapter.

Copy-protected files are turned away by name: Audible .aax, Kindle-store books and DRM’d epubs. Kindle-format files on a server get a one-line explanation and the offer to write the book from the audio instead.

14:36−12:40

Read a book with your friends.

Start a group, pick a book you’ve synced, and invite friends with a code. Everyone reads their own copy, in their own edition, and the group keeps pace together.

Stages and dates
The owner splits the book into stages that end at chapters, each with a date to reach it by. When a reader clears a stage, the group sees it.
Everyone’s place
The track shows where every reader is, from the start to the end, and who’s reading right now. A different edition is matched to the owner’s chapters.
Quotes
Highlight a passage and share it with the group. Friends see it in their own copy once they reach that chapter, so nothing is spoiled, and can play it in their own narrator’s voice or react with one of four emoji. Only where the passage is goes to the group, never its words or your notes.
Read or die
If the group turns it on, missing a stage’s date puts you out of the group until it starts its next book, unless the owner lets you back in. The owner plays by the same rule. No money, ever: just pride.
Invite by code
Send friends the 8-letter code or the link. They type the code, or paste the link, in the Groups tab.

What leaves your phone, and what never does.

There’s no account, no ads and no analytics of Witbound’s own. One feature talks to Witbound by default, community sync, and it shares timings, not books.

Stays on your device

  • Your ebooks, and your audiobooks too unless you turn on Cloud sync on Android
  • Your library and your reading stats
  • Highlights, notes and bookmarks, unless you share them or send them to your own server
  • Your settings

Goes online, and when

  • Community sync: word timings and fingerprints of the book, never its text, title or audio. There’s an off switch in Settings.
  • On Android, Cloud sync, if you turn it on: an English audiobook’s audio, in five-minute pieces over Wi-Fi, to Witbound’s server, which turns it into timed words and deletes it. Never your ebook.
  • Your place in each book, to your own iCloud, so your iPhone and iPad can pick up where the other left off. Witbound never sees it, and there’s an off switch in Settings.
  • Servers you connect yourself: your own audiobook server, ebook catalogue or media server, or a KOReader sync server.
  • Your purchase, through Apple or Google Play.
  • Anything you choose to share: a quote, a note, a progress card.
  • Discover, when you use it: the public Wanted and Synced lists and your searches in them, book covers, a book you add (looked up in Open Library or Google Books) and the free classics you get, from Project Gutenberg and the Internet Archive.
  • Your to-read list, and a book you’ve synced, only if you choose to share them.

Your book and its audioStay on the device. (On Android, Cloud sync sends the audio only if you turn it on, then deletes it.)

A timing mapWord positions and times. No text, no audio.

The next reader with the same editionTheir copy is ready in seconds.

If someone has already synced your edition, your copy is ready in seconds. Since version 1.8.2 Witbound also recognises the same book when the files aren’t identical: a friend’s copy, a different epub edition, or audio that was re-encoded. And when you tap Share on a book you’ve synced, it goes to the community straight away, so the next reader with that edition can find it in Discover.

On the App Store, Witbound’s privacy label lists Identifiers, Usage Data, User Content and Other Data as Data Linked to You, and Search History as not linked. What’s linked is Groups, and only if you join one: your group name and picture, a device key in place of an account, your place in the group’s book, the quotes you share there (where they are in the book, never its words), and the groups and books you add. Community sync’s timing maps and Discover’s lists still don’t say who you are. The privacy policy says exactly what is sent, and where.

The questions people ask first.

Will my Audible or Kindle books work?

No, and they never will. They’re copy-protected, and Witbound won’t break that. DRM-free books work: Libro.fm, LibriVox, Standard Ebooks and Project Gutenberg. New to this? Start with the free pairs.

What do I need?

An iPhone or iPad on iOS 26 or later, an ebook (epub or PDF) and its audiobook (m4b, m4a, mp3, flac, wav or aiff). Or connect your own audiobook server, ebook catalogue or media server. On Android 8 or later: an epub and its audiobook as m4b, m4a or mp3, from your files, Google Drive or the same servers.

How long does the first sync take?

You can start reading once chapter one is done, and the rest finishes in the background, overnight if you like. Popular editions are ready in seconds through community sync. The free Mac app syncs a long book in minutes.

What if my ebook and audiobook are different editions?

Witbound matches what it can and leaves the rest as ordinary text. It won’t force a bad match.

Do I need the internet?

Not to read or listen. Witbound goes online for community sync, which you can switch off, for Discover when you use it, for servers you connect, for purchases, and on Android for Cloud sync if you turn it on.

Can I reach my own server away from home?

Yes. Use Tailscale or any address you can reach, with an API key if you like. Self-signed certificates work: Witbound asks you to trust the server the first time.

Which languages?

English, French, Spanish, German, Italian, Portuguese, Japanese, Korean and Chinese, worked out from the book. Polish and more through Apple’s dictation, where the highlight can land a beat off more often.

What does it cost?

$9.99 once for every book. Your first book is free, and the Mac app is free.

Is there a Mac or Android version?

Mac: yes, free, for macOS 26 or later. Witbound for Mac syncs on the computer and sends the book to your iPhone or iPad. Android: yes, it runs on Android 8 or later and is on Google Play.

Where are my books stored?

On your device. Your ebooks are never uploaded, apart from a short passage you choose to share. On Android, if you turn on Cloud sync, an audiobook’s audio goes to Witbound’s server to be turned into timed words, and is deleted straight after. The privacy policy has the details.
