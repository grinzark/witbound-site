A fair comparison

Read-along apps for audiobooks you own, compared

You have the audiobook and the ebook, and you want the words to light up while the narrator reads. These are the serious ways to do that today, including the ones that aren’t Witbound.

By Moe, who makes Witbound. Last checked 1 October 2026. Apps change quickly: if something here is out of date, email me and I’ll fix it.

Amazon calls this immersion reading, or Read & Listen. Whatever the name, the promise is the same: your eyes and ears move through the book together, so your attention stays on the page. The apps below all get you some version of it. They differ in how closely the text follows the voice, whose books they accept, and what they ask of you in money, hardware and data.

Storyteller

A genuinely good project, and much more capable than it was a year ago. Since the v3 beta it aligns word by word, it has native iOS and Android apps, and hosted instances such as ElfHosted include the transcription hardware for you. It also lets you set separate speeds for reading along and for listening, which Witbound doesn’t have. If you like running services, or already pay for a host, it costs nothing and respects your ownership completely.

SoundLeaf and AudioBooth

If your audiobooks already live in Audiobookshelf and you want a full-featured player first, both are worth a look. SoundLeaf added word-by-word read-along with a matching epub served by Audiobookshelf. AudioBooth added sentence-level read-along with page turns. Both need the server.

Amazon’s Read & Listen

The most polished version of the idea, and the most expensive. Every book must be bought twice, once from Kindle and once from Audible, and the whole experience stays inside Amazon’s apps. If your library already lives there and the double price doesn’t bother you, it works well.

BookPlayer and Prologue

Lovely audiobook players. That is the point of them. If you never want text on screen, you may not need a read-along app at all.

Witbound

Witbound is for the case in between: you own DRM-free books, as plain files or on your own servers, you want each word lit as it’s spoken on the publisher’s own pages, and you would rather not run a server or make an account. Popular editions are ready in seconds through community sync. You pay once. If you’re new to this, start with the free public-domain pairs.

What Witbound doesn’t do

  • Open Audible or Kindle-store books. They’re copy-protected, and it won’t break that.
  • Set separate speeds for reading along and for listening.
  • Run on Android yet. It’s on its way.
  • Promise the same accuracy in every language. English is measured closely; most of the languages added in 1.8.0 are not yet.
